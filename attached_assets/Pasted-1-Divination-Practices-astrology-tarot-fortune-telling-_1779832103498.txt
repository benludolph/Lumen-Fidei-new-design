1. Divination
Practices: astrology, tarot, fortune-telling, omens, psychic readings.
Divination is opposed by Catholic teaching because it attempts to obtain hidden knowledge or control over the future apart from God’s providence. The Church teaches that Christians should place their trust in God rather than seek certainty through omens, astrology, psychics, or fortune-telling. Such practices can foster superstition, fear, and spiritual dependence on predictions and signs rather than God.
A person may become preoccupied with controlling the future rather than living in faith and prudence. The Church also warns that repeated involvement can distort moral judgment and openness to God’s will.
Examples:
Astrology and horoscopes
Tarot cards
Palm reading
Numerology
Rune casting
Pendulum divination
Tea leaf reading
Crystal ball gazing
I Ching divination
Fortune-telling
Scrying (mirrors, water, crystals)
Dowsing rods used for spiritual guidance

Key verses:
Deuteronomy 18:10–12
 “Let no one be found among you… who practices divination or sorcery, interprets omens, engages in witchcraft, or casts spells…”
Leviticus 19:26
 “Do not practice divination or seek omens.”
Isaiah 47:13–14
 Condemns astrologers and stargazers who claim to predict the future.
Jeremiah 10:2
 “Do not learn the ways of the nations or be terrified by signs in the heavens…”




2. Spirit Communication
Practices: séances, mediums, necromancy, Ouija boards.
Practices such as séances, mediumship, and attempts to contact the dead are rejected because Catholics are taught to seek spiritual guidance from God alone. The Church warns that attempting communication with spirits can open a person to deception and confusion, since not all spiritual phenomena come from God. The faithful are encouraged instead to pray for the dead and entrust them to God’s mercy.
Practices involving mediums or spirit contact may produce emotional distress, obsession with the dead, fear, or spiritual instability. They may also hinder healthy grieving and reliance on prayer and the sacraments.
Examples:
Séances
Mediumship
Necromancy
Channeling spirits
Ouija boards
Automatic writing
EVP (electronic voice phenomena) spirit communication
Ghost summoning rituals
Spirit guides
Ancestor invocation rituals
Key verses:
Leviticus 19:31
 “Do not turn to mediums or seek out spiritists…”
Leviticus 20:6
 God warns against consulting mediums and spirits.
Deuteronomy 18:11
 Specifically forbids consulting the dead.
1 Samuel 28
 Saul consults the medium of Endor and is judged for it.

3. Magic and Sorcery
Practices: spell casting, witchcraft, curses, ritual magic.
Magic and sorcery are condemned because they attempt to manipulate supernatural powers for personal gain, control, revenge, or hidden knowledge. Catholic teaching holds that supernatural power belongs to God alone and should never be treated as a tool to command or exploit. Even when presented as harmless or beneficial, magical practices can cultivate pride, manipulation, and spiritual disorder.
Magic and sorcery can cultivate a desire for power, control, revenge, or manipulation over others. These practices damage humility and encourage reliance on hidden powers. Spiritually, they may darken conscience, increase fear or obsession, and weaken a person’s relationship with God and others.

4. Satanic or Demonic Practices
Practices: demon invocation, satanic rituals, pacts.
Practices involving demons or Satan are considered gravely sinful because they directly oppose worship and obedience to God. Catholic teaching views demons as fallen angels who seek to deceive and separate people from God. Any ritual, invocation, or pact involving demonic forces is understood as spiritually dangerous and incompatible with Christian faith.
Demonic involvement is especially harmful because it intentionally opens a person to pure evil. Effects of these acts include deep fear, despair, hatred, spiritual bondage, and moral corruption. In severe cases, extraordinary demonic influence may result.
Examples:
Demon summoning
Satanic rituals
Black masses
Blood pacts
Demonic invocation
Occult initiation rites
Luciferian practices
Possession rituals
Key verses:
1 Corinthians 10:20–21
 “What pagans sacrifice they offer to demons and not to God.”
Ephesians 6:11–12
 Spiritual warfare against “powers” and “forces of evil.”
James 4:7
 “Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.”
1 Peter 5:8
 “Your adversary the devil prowls around like a roaring lion…”




5. Occult Objects and Talismans
Practices: amulets, charms, magical objects.
The use of charms, talismans, certain oils, and magical objects when they are believed to possess supernatural power is contrary to Christian faith. Catholicism distinguishes sacramentals—such as rosaries or medals blessed for prayer—from objects treated as magical instruments. Trusting in objects as sources of luck, protection, or power can become a form of superstition or idolatry.
Reliance on occult objects can foster superstition and psychological dependence on items believed to guarantee luck, protection, or power. The Church teaches that this displaces trust in God’s providence and can lead to fear when the object is absent or believed “cursed.” Such dependence may also reinforce irrational thinking and spiritual insecurity.
Examples:
Amulets
Talismans
Lucky charms
Cursed objects
Magical symbols
Spell jars
Protective occult jewelry
Ritual daggers and tools
Spirit boards
Fetishes used for magical purposes
Key verses:
Deuteronomy 7:25–26
 Israel warned not to keep cursed objects.
Acts 19:19
 Magic-related items/books destroyed after conversion.
Isaiah 2:6
 Condemns superstitious practices adopted from pagan cultures.

6. Esoteric and Secret Knowledge Systems
Practices: hidden spiritual knowledge, occult initiation systems.
Systems claiming hidden spiritual knowledge for an elite group are viewed critically because Catholicism teaches that God’s essential revelation is openly given through Christ and the Church. The Church cautions against spiritual pride, secret doctrines, or teachings that replace divine revelation with hidden enlightenment. Salvation is not achieved through secret knowledge but through grace, faith, and communion with God.
These systems may encourage spiritual pride and elitism. This can isolate a person from an authentic Christian community and distort understanding of truth. The pursuit of secret enlightenment may also lead to confusion, relativism, or loss of faith.
Examples:
Hermeticism
Gnosticism
Alchemy (spiritual/esoteric forms)
Kabbalah
Rosicrucianism
Theosophy
Freemasonry
Secret initiation societies
Key verses:
Colossians 2:8
 “See to it that no one takes you captive through hollow and deceptive philosophy…”
1 Timothy 6:20
 Warning against “false knowledge.”
Genesis 3:4–5
 The serpent tempts humanity with hidden godlike knowledge.

7. New Age and Energy Practices
Practices: channeling, crystals, spiritual energy manipulation.
Many New Age and energy-based practices are considered problematic when they invoke impersonal spiritual forces, channel entities, or promote incompatible spiritual worldviews. Catholic teaching emphasizes a personal relationship with God rather than manipulation of cosmic energies or self-deification. The concern is especially strong when these practices replace prayer, sacraments, or dependence on God.
New Age practices can blur the distinction between God, humanity, and creation, encouraging self-centered spirituality or relativism. Individuals may become spiritually confused by mixing incompatible beliefs. The Church is especially concerned when these practices replace prayer, moral conversion, and dependence on God.
Examples:
Channeling ascended masters
Aura reading
Chakra manipulation
Reiki
Crystal healing
Energy healing invoking spiritual forces
Astral projection
Third-eye awakening
Manifestation rituals with occult intent
Key verses often applied by Catholics:
Isaiah 8:19
 “Should not a people inquire of their God? Why consult the dead on behalf of the living?”
Jeremiah 17:5
 Warning against placing ultimate trust in human/spiritual systems apart from God.
Colossians 2:18
 Warning against false spirituality and mystical speculation.

8. Altered Consciousness and Mystical Techniques
Practices: trance rituals, possession ceremonies, occult ecstatic states.
The Church warns against trance states, occult ecstatic practices, or techniques aimed at surrendering consciousness to unknown spiritual influences. Catholic spirituality values self-control, discernment, and prayer rooted in God’s grace. Practices designed to bypass reason or invite uncontrolled spiritual experiences may expose individuals to psychological or spiritual harm.
Occult trance practices may impair discernment, emotional balance, and self-control. Catholic spirituality emphasizes sobriety and conscious cooperation with grace, whereas techniques aimed at surrendering the mind to unknown influences may create vulnerability to psychological distress or deceptive spiritual experiences. Some individuals may also develop escapist habits or detachment from reality.
Examples:
Trance rituals
Shamanic journeying
Spirit possession ceremonies
Drug-assisted occult rituals
Automatic trance states
Certain forms of ecstatic ritual practice
Key verses:
1 Peter 5:8
 “Be sober-minded; be watchful.”
Ephesians 5:18
 “Do not get drunk on wine… instead be filled with the Spirit.”
2 Timothy 1:7
 God gives “a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control.”

9. Folk Magic and Superstition
Practices: curses, evil eye rituals, lucky charms.
Folk magic and superstition are discouraged because they place trust in rituals, omens, or objects rather than in God’s providence. Even culturally accepted practices can become spiritually harmful when they encourage fear, manipulation, or belief in magical causation. Catholic teaching calls believers to live by faith and trust in God rather than superstition.
Superstitious practices can create chronic fear, anxiety, and compulsive behavior, especially when a person believes everyday events are controlled by curses, omens, or magical forces. Catholic teaching holds that superstition undermines freedom and trust in divine providence. It may also perpetuate manipulation within families or cultures through fear-based beliefs.
Examples:
Folk curses
Evil eye rituals
Superstitious charms
Magical blessings outside religion
Voodoo spellwork
Hoodoo practices
Brujería
Santería elements involving magic/divination
Lucky rituals believed to manipulate fate
Key verses:
Leviticus 19:26
 Prohibits omens and superstitious divination.
Isaiah 44:9–20
 Ridicules trust in magical or man-made spiritual objects.
Matthew 6:33
 Trust God first rather than superstitious practices.

10. Occult Religious Systems
Practices: religions centered on magic, invocation, occult worship.
Religious systems centered on magic, occult power, or the worship of spiritual beings apart from God conflict with Catholic belief in the one God revealed through Jesus Christ. The Church teaches that worship belongs to God alone and rejects religious practices involving occult rituals, magical invocation, or alternative spiritual authorities. Participation in such systems is seen as incompatible with Christian discipleship.
Participation in occult-centered religions can gradually reshape a person’s worldview away from Christian teaching and sacramental life. The Church teaches that such systems may normalize practices contrary to Christian morality and worship. Spiritually, this can lead to alienation from God, confusion about truth, and difficulty maintaining a coherent Christian faith and identity.
Examples:
Wicca
Satanism
Luciferianism
Certain forms of pagan ritualism
Thelema
Esoteric occult orders
Neo-pagan magical traditions
Key verses:
Exodus 20:3–5
 “You shall have no other gods before me.”
Deuteronomy 12:29–31
 Israel forbidden from adopting pagan worship practices.
Revelation 21:8
 Sorcerers included among those judged by God.
1 Corinthians 10:14
 “Flee from idolatry.”

Occult Third-Eye
The “third eye” is an occult power that comes from a demon, referring to an inner spiritual eye, hidden consciousness, or awakened supernatural perception. It is commonly associated with practices involving chakras, kundalini awakening, esoteric meditation, and mystical enlightenment found in some forms of Hinduism, Buddhism, and New Age spirituality.
Spiritual enlightenment or “divine” knowledge attained through hidden inner powers or energies is spiritually dangerous. Because, these practices can become gateways to spiritual deception and invite a person to seek supernatural experiences apart from God. Deliberate attempts to awaken the “third eye” may expose individuals to demonic influence, oppression, confusion, fear, or counterfeit mystical experiences masquerading as enlightenment.
Demons will imitate spiritual experiences to draw people away from Christ and toward pride, self-deification, occult dependence, or false spirituality. Catholic spirituality, instead, emphasizes humility, discernment, sacramental life, prayer, and trust in God alone.

Yoga
Its religious roots are non-Christian
Yoga originated as a spiritual discipline within Hinduism and related Eastern traditions. Classical yoga was designed to lead a practitioner toward spiritual enlightenment, encourage inward self-divinization, emptying the mind, altered consciousness, or union with an impersonal divine reality. 
Traditional yoga postures (“asanas”), chants, and meditations were historically connected to acts of spiritual discipline or devotion. Some Catholic critics argue that participation in practices originally ordered toward non-Christian worship can gradually shape a person spiritually, even when approached casually.
It may introduce incompatible beliefs
Many forms of yoga incorporate concepts such as:
chakras
kundalini energy
karma
reincarnation
divine consciousness within the self
Catholic teaching rejects the idea that spiritual enlightenment is achieved by awakening hidden energies or discovering one’s own divinity. Christianity teaches that God is distinct from creation and that salvation is a gift of grace, not self-realization.

Buddhism, Mindfulness, Centering Prayer, and False Meditation
Concerns about mindfulness practices, Buddhism, and forms of Eastern meditation derive from their spiritual frameworks and the worldview they may promote.
Authentic Catholic prayer leads to a personal relationship with God through Jesus Christ, involving attention, love, repentance, and openness to grace. Forms of meditation associated with Eastern religions or secular adaptations of them, shift the focus away from God as a person and toward techniques of mental emptying, self-awareness, or union with a collective conscience or the universe.
The Church warns us that such techniques:
blur the distinction between Christian prayer and non-Christian spirituality
lead to confusion about the nature of God and salvation
encourage reliance on technique rather than grace
foster self-centered or inward-focused spirituality rather than relationship with God
These meditation methods aim at detachment from thoughts, desires, or the self in a way that can resemble non-theistic or monistic spiritual systems. The goal of Catholic meditation is not to empty the mind into nothingness, but to encounter God, who is personal, loving, and distinct from creation.
