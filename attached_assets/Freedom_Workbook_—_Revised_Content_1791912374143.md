# Freedom Workbook — Revised Content

Oct 13, 2026 · @Ben

## The shift

The workbook moves from inventory to encounter. The current version asks participants to find and list everything, then renounce each item and every spirit that might be attached to it. The revised version asks the Holy Spirit to bring to light what He wants healed now, explains why it matters and what the Church teaches, and sends sin to Confession and wounds to healing.

What changes, drawn from *Big God, Little Devil* (paraphrased throughout, no quotations in participant-facing copy):

- **Starting point.** Christ's victory is already won. Participants work from freedom toward deeper freedom, and the tone moves from vigilance to trust.
- **Focus.** Christ and the Father's love stay at the center. The enemy is named where needed and briefly. Tobin warns that a community preoccupied with the devil loses its power to give hope.
- **Method.** In Tobin's cases, lasting change followed the person's encounter with God's love. Sections invite reflection and do not audit.
- **Roots.** A pattern of sin usually protects or provides something. Sections ask what the participant was looking for, so the need underneath comes into view and can be brought to God.
- **Agency.** The enemy cannot make anyone sin. Participants own their choices and learn they are not helpless.
- **Completion.** Freedom holds when nothing is left for the enemy to return to: no agreement with a lie, no unhealed wound, no part of the person unsure of being loved. Every lie named receives a truth to replace it.

### Three things every participant learns

1. **A sin and a wound are different.** A sin is what I chose. A wound is what was done to me. Sins go to Confession. Wounds go to the Lord for healing and are never confessed as my sin.
2. **Grave and venial sin are different.** Every grave sin is confessed by kind and number (CCC 1456). Confessing venial sins is recommended (CCC 1458). Grave matter becomes mortal sin only with full knowledge and deliberate consent (CCC 1857–1859), and the priest can help sort out what applies. Each section states plainly which of its contents are grave matter.
3. **Remembering everything is not the participant's burden.** An honest, careful examination is enough. A sin forgotten in good faith is forgiven with the rest, and if it comes to mind later, it goes to the next Confession.

### How each section is built

Every section has the same five parts: why it matters, what the Church teaches, what to bring to Confession, an invitation, and the fields. The invitation always begins by asking the Lord what He wants to show, and it ends the effort: write what comes, then stop.

## For participants who are not Catholic

Participants who are not Catholic get the same workbook with a different path for sin. Catholics bring sin to Confession. Everyone else confesses to God directly and is encouraged to confess serious sin to a trusted pastor or mature believer (1 John 1:9; James 5:16).

**Profile question.** At signup: "Are you Catholic?" Options: "Yes" · "No, I'm a Christian of another tradition" · "No, I'm exploring faith." The last two follow the same path.

**Teaching label.** The teaching paragraph in every section is labeled "What the Catholic Church teaches" for all participants. A non-Catholic reads it as the program's teaching, offered to them, with no assumption that they hold it.

**Grave and venial.** For non-Catholics, the second of the three things every participant learns reads: "Some sins are more serious than others (1 John 5:16–17). Name serious sins specifically, by kind, when you confess them."

**Replacement for every "Bring to Confession" block.** The heading becomes "Bring to the Lord," followed by this text:

> Name each of these to God plainly and ask His forgiveness. He is faithful to forgive (1 John 1:9). Serious sin is worth also speaking aloud to a pastor or a believer you trust, because confession to one another brings healing (James 5:16). If you would like to talk with a Catholic priest, the ministry team can arrange it.

## Movement 1: Where I have turned away

**Category description:** The choices that turned me from God, and what I was looking for when I made them.

### Sins to bring to Confession (section 1)

*Absorbs 9 Pride, Rebellion & Control and 18 Agreement Sins.*

**Why it matters.** Sin is a turning away from a Father who loves you, and it wounds your friendship with Him and with others. Seeing it clearly is the first step home, the moment the prodigal son came to himself (Luke 15:17). The Father was already watching the road.

**What the Church teaches.** Sin is a failure of real love for God and neighbor (CCC 1849). The Church examines conscience by the Ten Commandments and the Beatitudes (CCC 1454). Pride, greed, envy, anger, lust, gluttony, and sloth are called capital sins because other sins grow from them (CCC 1866). We also share in another person's sin when we encourage it, approve it, or fail to stop it when we could and should have (CCC 1868).

**Bring to Confession.** Grave matter here includes missing Sunday Mass without a serious reason (CCC 2181), serious lies or damage to someone's reputation, theft, deliberate hatred, and helping someone obtain an abortion (CCC 2272). Name these by kind and roughly how often. Everyday impatience, unkindness, and neglected prayer are venial, and confessing them is how grace reaches the habits underneath.

**Invitation.** Ask the Holy Spirit to show you where you have turned away from God or from others. If it helps, take one Commandment at a time. Write each thing plainly, and if you can see it, write what you were looking for. Stop when the Lord stops showing you.

**Fields**

- `sin` — required, textarea. Label: "Something to bring to Confession."
- `how` — optional, enum. Label: "How was I involved?" Options: "I did this" (default) · "I encouraged, approved, or went along with it."
- `seeking` — optional, textarea. Label: "What was I looking for?" Placeholder: "e.g. to feel in control, to be noticed, to escape."

### Sexuality and chastity (section 4)

**Why it matters.** God made sexuality for a complete gift of self in love. Used apart from that gift, it divides what He meant to join and leaves bonds that outlast the act. Nothing you write here shocks Him, and He wants your dignity restored.

**What the Church teaches.** Chastity is the integration of sexuality within the whole person (CCC 2337). Lust, masturbation, fornication, pornography, and prostitution are offenses against chastity (CCC 2351–2355). Adultery and contraceptive acts are offenses against marriage (CCC 2380, 2370).

**Bring to Confession.** These are grave matter. Name the kind and roughly how often or how long, for example "pornography, several times a week, for about two years." The priest does not need details.

**Abuse is a wound.** Sexual abuse you suffered is not your sin, and it belongs in Movement 5 under Wounds I carry. If confusion after abuse led you into choices of your own, bring them gently; fear, habit, and psychological factors can lessen responsibility (CCC 1735, 2352).

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord to show you where your sexuality has been used apart from love. Name relationships and habits in a few words. If a person is connected, add their name so the bond can be released in prayer.

**Fields**

- `sin` — required, textarea. Label: "What I need to bring to the Lord."
- `person_or_partner` — optional, text. Label: "Person connected."

### What I run to (section 11)

*Absorbs 8 Idolatry.*

**Why it matters.** Everyone runs somewhere when they are hurting, afraid, or empty. When that place becomes the first refuge instead of God, it begins to rule. Most idols and addictions are ordinary comforts or good things asked to do what only God can do.

**What the Church teaches.** Idolatry is giving a created thing the place that belongs to God; money, power, pleasure, and approval can all become idols (CCC 2113). The virtue of temperance calls us to avoid abuse of food, alcohol, and drugs (CCC 2290–2291). The Church also recognizes that habit and psychological factors weaken freedom (CCC 1735, 2352), which is why the way out includes grace, accountability, and often professional help.

**Bring to Confession.** Grave matter includes recreational drug use (CCC 2291), drunkenness that removes reason or endangers others (CCC 2290), and gambling that deprives your family of what it needs (CCC 2413). Confess an idol of money, a person, or approval when it led you to neglect God or harm someone. With a habitual sin, keep returning; frequent Confession builds the strength the habit took.

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord: "When I am hurting, where do I go first?" Write each thing, then write what it gives you or helps you avoid feeling. That second answer usually points to the need God wants to meet Himself.

**Fields**

- `addiction` — required, textarea. Label: "What I run to."
- `what_it_gives` — optional, textarea. Label: "What it gives me or helps me avoid." Placeholder: "e.g. quiet, worth, relief from loneliness."

### People I have hurt (section 13)

**Why it matters.** Wounds we received are easy to remember, and wounds we caused are easy to forget. Owning the harm you did frees you and can open the way for the other person's healing.

**What the Church teaches.** Justice asks us to repair harm where we can: return what was taken (CCC 2412) and restore a reputation we damaged (CCC 2487). Prudence matters too. An apology that would reopen harm for the other person or put anyone at risk may not be the right step, and a priest can help you discern.

**Bring to Confession.** Name the harm by kind. Ask the priest whether amends are owed and what form they should take.

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord whose face He brings to mind. Write the person and what you did. Mark any place where you think an apology or restitution may be owed.

**Fields**

- `harm` — required, textarea. Label: "Who I hurt and how."
- `amends_owed` — optional, boolean. Label: "I may owe an apology or restitution here."

## Movement 2: Seeking power apart from God

**Category description:** Where I looked for guidance, protection, or power somewhere other than God.

### Occult, false worship, and spiritual mixture (section 5)

*Absorbs 12 False Religion, 19 Named Spirits, and the spells and pacts from 21.*

**Why it matters.** People usually come to these practices looking for something real: guidance, protection, healing, control over an uncertain future, or contact with someone they lost. God wants to give those things Himself. Naming where you looked elsewhere returns that trust to Him, and Christ's authority over every one of these powers is already complete (Colossians 2:15).

**What the Church teaches.** The First Commandment calls us to worship and trust God alone. All divination is rejected, including horoscopes, astrology, palm reading, tarot, mediums, and consulting the dead, because it seeks control over time and people apart from God (CCC 2116). Magic, sorcery, spiritism, and charms are gravely contrary to the virtue of religion (CCC 2117), and offering worship or devotion to other gods or spirits is idolatry (CCC 2112–2113). The U.S. bishops have advised Catholics against Reiki (USCCB, 2009).

**Bring to Confession.** Deliberate involvement is grave matter against the First Commandment. Name the practice and roughly when. Things done as a young child or without understanding carry less responsibility, or none; bring them anyway so they can be renounced, and the priest will guide you. If you ever made a pact, offered blood, or dedicated yourself to a spirit, tell the priest, who may connect you with the diocesan exorcist.

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord where you looked for power, knowledge, or protection apart from Him. Write each practice. If a spirit, deity, or being was named or called on, write that too.

**Fields**

- `practice` — required, textarea. Label: "What I did or took part in."
- `kind` — optional, enum. Label: "Kind." Options: "Practice or participation" (default) · "A spell, curse, or pact I made."
- `spirit_named` — optional, text. Label: "Spirit, deity, or being called on."

### Objects in my home (section 20)

**Why it matters.** Repentance often takes a physical step. In Ephesus, new believers burned their books of magic in public (Acts 19:19). Removing an object closes a door you opened and shows that your heart has chosen.

**What the Church teaches.** The Church calls the wearing of charms reprehensible (CCC 2117). Objects are not to be feared as though they held power of their own, which would itself be superstition (CCC 2111). They are removed because they belong to a practice or allegiance you are leaving behind.

**Bring to Confession.** The object itself usually needs no confession; the practice it served belongs in the section above.

**Invitation.** Walk through your home and your devices once, asking the Lord what does not belong. List each object. When you have removed them, a priest can bless your home.

**Fields**

- `object` — required, textarea. Label: "Object."
- `plan` — optional, enum. Label: "What I will do." Options: "Destroy or throw away" · "Ask a priest about it" · "Already removed."

## Movement 3: What I inherited

**Category description:** What my family passed to me, what I want to end with me, and what I want to hand on.

### Patterns in my family line (section 15)

**Why it matters.** Most families carry patterns that repeat across generations, such as heavy drinking, rage, divorce, silence, or despair. You did not choose them, and you are not guilty of your ancestors' sins (Ezekiel 18:20). Patterns still shape a family, and naming one is how it begins to stop with you.

**What the Church teaches.** Personal guilt does not pass from parent to child. Original sin is a wounded state we inherit, not a personal fault (CCC 404–405). The Church does recognize that sin shapes the situations and structures others live in (CCC 408, 1869), and Scripture pairs the warning about generations with mercy to thousands (Exodus 20:5–6).

**Bring to Confession.** Nothing your family did is yours to confess. Where you have taken part in a pattern yourself, that belongs in Movement 1.

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord to show you what runs on your father's side and your mother's side. Name what you already know; research is not required. If you were adopted, both families count.

**Fields**

- `pattern_or_sin` — required, textarea. Label: "Pattern."
- `side` — optional, enum. Label: "Where it shows up." Options: "Father's side" · "Mother's side" · "Both sides" · "Adoptive family."

### Family members of concern (section 16)

*Absorbs 17 Lodges, Secret Societies & Oath-Bound Groups.*

**Why it matters.** Sometimes one person's choices cast a long shadow, such as an ancestor involved in the occult, a secret society, or a vow made over the family. Naming them is intercession. You are standing before God on their behalf.

**What the Church teaches.** Membership in Masonic associations remains forbidden, and Catholics who join are in a state of grave sin and may not receive Holy Communion (CDF, Declaration on Masonic Associations, 1983). The Church prays for the dead and commends them to God's mercy (CCC 1032).

**Bring to Confession.** If you belonged to such a group yourself, confess it and tell the priest. For a relative, you pray and renounce on the family's behalf; there is nothing for you to confess.

**Invitation.** Name the person and what concerns you. If they belonged to a lodge or oath-bound group, name it and any degree or rank you know. If the member was you, write "myself."

**Fields**

- `name_or_description` — required, text. Label: "Who."
- `groups_or_sins_involved` — optional, textarea. Label: "What concerns me."
- `group` — optional, text. Label: "Lodge, society, or oath-bound group, with degree if known."

### The family dead (section 25)

**Why it matters.** Some members of a family die in ways that leave a wound behind, and some are never mourned at all. Praying for them is love reaching back through time.

**What the Church teaches.** The Church has always prayed for the dead and offered Mass for them (CCC 1032). For those who took their own lives, the Church does not despair of their salvation and prays for them (CCC 2283). Children who died before baptism are entrusted to God's mercy (CCC 1261).

**Bring to Confession.** If you had an abortion or helped someone obtain one, bring it to Confession; any priest can absolve it. Here you name and mourn the child. Project Rachel offers confidential post-abortion healing through the Church.

**Invitation.** Go through both sides of your family as far as you know. Name those who died by suicide, violence, accident, or war; those who died young, alone, or unmourned; and every child lost to miscarriage or abortion, including your own. If a child was never named, you may give them a name.

**Fields:** unchanged (`name`, `circumstance`, `notes`, `named_now`).

### Body and mind (section 22)

**Why it matters.** You can bring your body and mind to the Lord as they are. Jesus healed throughout His ministry, and He still heals through prayer, the sacraments, and medical care.

**What the Church teaches.** Illness can lead a person toward anguish or toward God (CCC 1501). The Church does not ask you to find a spiritual cause behind every illness. The Anointing of the Sick is for anyone facing serious illness or the frailty of age (CCC 1514).

**Bring to Confession.** Nothing here is sin. If an illness is tied to a habit of your own, that habit belongs in Movement 1.

**Invitation.** Name what you carry in body and mind. Note anything that began after a specific event. Keep following your doctor's care as you pray.

**Fields:** unchanged (`infirmity`, `for_healing`).

### Blessing for those after me (section 23)

**Why it matters.** Blessing passes down a family line too. Having named what you do not want to pass on, you can ask for what you do want for those who come after you.

**What the Church teaches.** Parents are the first to hand on the faith (CCC 2223–2226), and every baptized person is called to bless (CCC 1669).

**Invitation.** Name what you want to end with you and the blessing you ask for in its place. If you have no children, pray for nieces, nephews, godchildren, or those you mentor.

**Fields**

- `concern` — required, textarea. Label: "What ends with me."
- `blessing` — optional, textarea. Label: "The blessing I ask for them."

## Movement 4: What I came to believe

**Category description:** The lies I accepted, the promises I made to protect myself, and the bonds that still pull at me.

### Lies and the truth (section 6)

*Absorbs the curses spoken over me from 21.*

**Why it matters.** A single believed sentence can govern a life: "No one sees me." "I am helpless." "God is disappointed in me." Lies like these usually arrive through a wound and stay because they once explained the pain. Freedom holds when each lie is replaced with what God says is true, the way Hagar met the God who sees her (Genesis 16:13).

**What the Church teaches.** Jesus calls the devil the father of lies (John 8:44). His power is real and limited, and it cannot stop the building of God's kingdom (CCC 395). The truth is that you are made in God's image and called to share His life (CCC 356–357) and became His adopted child in baptism (CCC 1265). The Church also asks us to purify the false images of God we carry, including those drawn from our own fathers (CCC 2779).

**Bring to Confession.** A lie you believed is usually a wound, so the belief itself is not confessed. Confess what you chose because of it when it harmed you or others. Despair is a sin against hope when a person deliberately stops hoping for God's mercy (CCC 2091), and it can be confessed.

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord: "What have I believed about myself, about You, or about others that is not true?" Write each lie the way it sounds inside you, including words spoken over you and words you have said against yourself. Note who said it first, if anyone. Then find the truth in Scripture that answers it and write it in your own words.

**Fields:** unchanged (`lie`, `spoken_by`, `lying_spirit`, `scripture_reference`, `scripture_text`, `truth_statement`). Change the UI label for `lying_spirit` from "Lying spirit" to "What the lie says."

### Vows I made (section 7)

**Why it matters.** In moments of pain, people make quiet promises: "I'll never need anyone." "I'll never let them see me cry." They felt like protection. Over time they put the wound in charge of choices that belong to you and to God.

**What the Church teaches.** "Inner vow" is a term from Christian healing ministry and has no formal definition in Church teaching. The principle underneath is the Church's teaching on freedom: we grow freer each time we choose the good, and choices made from fear or hatred bind us (CCC 1731–1733).

**Bring to Confession.** Most vows are a wounded response and are not confessed. A vow of revenge or hatred that you have deliberately kept can be confessed.

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord to bring to mind any "I will never" or "I will always" you said in a hard moment. Write it as you said it. If you remember when, note it.

**Fields**

- `vow` — required, textarea. Label: "What I promised myself."
- `when_made` — optional, text. Label: "When I made it."

### Bonds that still hold me (section 24)

**Why it matters.** Some relationships leave a pull that outlasts them. You find yourself rehearsing conversations, needing someone's approval, or unable to move past them even after forgiving. Naming that pull lets the Lord set your love back in order.

**What the Church teaches.** "Soul tie" is a term from healing ministry and has no formal definition in Church teaching. The Church teaches that to love is to will the good of another (CCC 1766), and love of that kind leaves both people free. A bond built on control, fear, sexual sin, or dependence works against that freedom.

**Bring to Confession.** The bond itself is not confessed. If it still draws you into sin, such as ongoing contact that leads to sexual sin, that belongs in Movement 1.

**Invitation.** Ask the Holy Spirit to show you who still has a hold on you. Write the person, how the bond formed, and whether you still feel its pull.

**Fields:** unchanged (`person_or_relationship`, `how_formed`, `still_pulls_at_me`).

## Movement 5: Where I was wounded

**Category description:** What was done to me, who I am releasing, and the thread that runs through it all.

### Wounds I carry (section 3)

*Absorbs 10 Abuse, Trauma & Wounded Responses.*

**Why it matters.** Wounds from childhood or later shape how you see yourself, God, and other people. Most wounds also taught you a way to cope, such as going numb, taking control, or keeping everyone at a distance. Those ways once protected you, and the Lord wants to meet you in the place they were built to guard.

**What the Church teaches.** What was done to you is not your sin. The Church recognizes that fear, duress, and psychological factors reduce responsibility for what a person does afterward (CCC 1735). Spiritual healing and professional care work together.

**Bring to Confession.** Nothing done to you is confessed. Responses that became sin, such as hatred kept on purpose, revenge, or using others, can be brought to Confession gently.

**Care note.** If you are working through severe trauma, walk this section with a priest, counselor, or therapist. If a memory becomes overwhelming, stop and reach out to someone you trust.

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord which wound He wants to heal first. You do not need to relive the details. Write what happened, the message you took from it, and how you learned to protect yourself. If you sense anything of His presence in that memory, write that too.

**Fields**

- `whose` — required, enum. Label: "Source." Options: "Father" · "Mother" · "Family / Childhood" · "Someone else" · "An event."
- `what` — required, textarea. Label: "What happened."
- `lie_believed` — optional, textarea. Label: "The message I took from it."
- `coping` — optional, textarea. Label: "How I learned to protect myself."
- `presence` — optional, textarea. Label: "What the Lord is showing me."

### People I am releasing (section 2)

**Why it matters.** Forgiveness releases a debt you are owed into God's hands. It is a choice made with grace, and feelings often follow later. It does not require reconciliation, renewed trust, or calling what happened acceptable.

**What the Church teaches.** Jesus ties the mercy we receive to the mercy we give (Matthew 6:14–15). The Church acknowledges that we cannot always stop feeling or forget an injury, and teaches that a heart open to the Holy Spirit can turn hurt into compassion and prayer for the other person (CCC 2843). Deliberately wishing serious harm on someone is grave matter (CCC 2303).

**On God and yourself.** God has done no wrong and needs no forgiveness. You can still bring your anger and disappointment to Him honestly, as the psalms do, and let go of your accusation against Him. With yourself, the work is receiving the forgiveness God has already given.

**Bring to Confession.** Feeling hurt is not a sin. A deliberate refusal to forgive, or hatred you keep on purpose, can be confessed.

**Invitation.** Ask the Lord who He wants you to release. Write the person and what they did. You may include yourself and your anger at God.

**Fields**

- `person` — required, text. Label: "Who."
- `who_kind` — optional, enum. Label: "This is." Options: "Someone who hurt me" (default) · "Myself" · "My anger at God."
- `what` — optional, textarea. Label: "What happened."
- `is_family_harm` — unchanged.

### The thread (section 14)

*Moved from Movement 1.*

**Why it matters.** Looking back over everything you wrote, a few themes usually repeat in different forms. Seeing the thread lets you bring the root to God instead of fighting each symptom on its own.

**Invitation.** Read back over what you wrote in all five movements. Choose the two or three themes that run through it. Then write one sentence: what you most need to hear from the Father.

**Fields**

- `checked` — checklist, trimmed to: Fear · Rejection · Shame · Control · Pride · Anger · Bitterness · Lust · Addiction · Despair · Self-hatred · Confusion · Unbelief · Occult involvement · Grief · Isolation · Other.
- `other_freetext` — unchanged.
- `father_word` — optional, textarea. Label: "What I most need to hear from the Father."
